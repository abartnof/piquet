# Introduction

**What it is:** A classic two-player French trick-taking game, played over 6 deals (a *partie*).

**Cards:** A 32-card deck (7 through Ace in each suit). Each player gets 12 cards, leaving 8 in the deck (the *talon*). In each deal, one player deals, and the other player — the non-dealer, called the *elder hand* — has an advantage, because they get to act first. The dealer is the *younger hand*.

**Goal:** Score more points than your opponent across all deals, through the phases of the game.

## The phases of each deal

1. **Exchange:** Each player discards a few cards and draws the same number of cards, in an effort to get a pretty good hand.
2. **Declarations:** Players announce and compare their best combinations. The higher one scores, and the loser scores nothing in that category. You may lie.
3. **Play of tricks:** You use all the cards in your hand in a fast-paced series of tricks.

## Bonuses

- **Pique:** If the elder hand reaches 30 points before the opponent scores anything, they get 30 extra points.
- **Repique:** Reaching 30 points from declarations alone, before the opponent scores anything, earns 60 extra points.

For both, points count in round order — point, sequences, sets, then tricks — even though the younger hand announces their declarations after the first lead.

After all deals, the higher total wins the difference plus 100. If the loser didn't reach 100 points (the *rubicon*), the winner takes both totals plus 100.

# The Exchange

**How it works:** After the deal, each player has 12 cards and 8 sit face-down in the talon. The top 5 are reserved for the elder hand (non-dealer) and the bottom 3 for the younger hand (dealer).

1. **Elder hand:** Discards 1 to 5 cards face-down and draws the same number from the top of the talon.
2. **Younger hand:** Discards 1 to 3 cards, or more if the elder hand left some untaken, and draws the same number. If the elder hand took only 3 cards, the younger hand can draw up to 5.
3. **Peeking:** If the elder hand takes fewer than 5 cards, they may look at the ones they left.

Discards stay private, but both players can see how many cards the other exchanged, which hints at hand strength.

## What to look for in a good hand

- **Many cards of the same suit.** Aim for 5 or more.
- **Runs of 3 or more consecutive cards in one suit**, such as 9-8-7 or K-Q-J. Longer runs and higher cards are worth more, and the cards also add to your total in that suit.
- **Three or four cards of the same rank, 10 or higher.** Remember, aces are high.
- **High cards generally**, since aces and kings win tricks later.

## What to avoid

- **Holding scattered low cards "just in case."** Low cards spread across different suits with nothing linking them rarely help, so discard them.
- **Breaking up a group of cards in one suit** to chase same-rank cards. Many cards in one suit, especially with a run, are usually worth more.

## Quick strategy tips

- As elder hand, exchange a full 5 when your hand is weak. It gives you the best shot at improving.
- As elder hand, remember that every card you leave is available to your opponent. Leaving cards is a trade-off, not free safety.
- As younger hand, watch how many cards the elder hand took. A small exchange suggests a strong hand, and it also leaves you extra cards to draw from.
- Decide first what you're aiming for (many cards in one suit, a run, or same-rank cards), then discard everything that doesn't support it.

# The Declarations

**How it works:** After the exchange, both players compare their hands in three rounds, always in this order: **point**, then **sequences**, then **sets**. In each round, the player with the better hand scores, and the other player scores nothing; if they are exactly equal, neither scores. Aces are high.

## Round 1: The point

Your longest suit, meaning the most cards of one suit in your hand (like a flush in poker).

- **Score:** 1 point per card in that suit.
- **Who wins:** The player with more cards in the suit. Ties go to the suit with the higher total value: aces count 11, face cards 10, and all other cards their number.

## Round 2: The sequences

A run of 3 or more consecutive cards in one suit, such as 9-8-7 or K-Q-J (like a straight flush in poker).

- **Score:** 3 cards score 3, 4 cards score 4, 5 cards score 15, 6 cards score 16, 7 cards score 17, and 8 cards score 18.
- **Who wins:** The longer run. If the runs are the same length, the run with the higher top card wins.
- **Bonus:** If you win, you also score any other sequences in your hand.

## Round 3: The sets

Three or four cards of the same rank, 10 or higher (like three or four of a kind in poker).

- **Score:** Three of a kind scores 3, and four of a kind scores 14.
- **Who wins:** Four beats three. If both have the same number, the higher rank wins.
- **Bonus:** If you win, you also score any other sets in your hand.

## Lying

Feel free to bluff, but only downward: you can claim one card fewer than you're holding, or nothing at all, but never more. Got five hearts? You can say you only have four.

- **The cost:** You only score what you claim, so you give up points.
- **The payoff:** Your opponent has a harder time figuring out what's in your hand once the tricks begin.

# The Tricks

**How it works:** After the declarations, you play out all 12 cards, one trick at a time. The elder hand leads first by playing any card. Then the other player responds, and the higher card wins. Aces are high, and whoever wins a trick leads the next one.

**Follow suit if you can.** If your opponent leads a heart, you must play a heart if you have one. Only cards of the led suit can win the trick. If you have no cards in that suit, you can play anything, but your card automatically loses, even if it's an ace. There's no trump suit, so nothing beats the suit that was led.

## Scoring

- **Leading:** 1 point each time you lead a card, whatever it is.
- **Following:** 1 point each time you win a trick your opponent led.
- **Last trick:** 1 extra point for winning it.
- **Most tricks:** 10 points for winning 7 or more of the 12. If you split 6-6, nobody gets it.
- **Capot:** Win all 12 tricks and you score 40 instead of the 10.

## Things to try

- **Lead from your longest suit.** Once your opponent runs out of it, your remaining cards in that suit are guaranteed winners.
- **Lead high cards early.** An ace or king you lead will probably win the trick, and winning keeps you on lead, where every card you lead scores a point.
- **Track what's been played.** With only 32 cards, you can often work out what your opponent is still holding.
- **Go for the majority.** It's worth 10 points, far more than any single trick, so think in terms of winning 7 tricks, not every trick.

## Things to avoid

- **Throwing away your best cards.** If a trick is already lost, play your weakest card and keep your aces and kings for tricks you can actually win.
- **Chasing every trick.** Winning a trick with your best card can leave you with nothing but weak cards later.
- **Expecting a high card to win in the wrong suit.** If you're out of the led suit, even your ace can't win, so don't count on it.
